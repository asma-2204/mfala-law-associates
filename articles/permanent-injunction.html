Permanent Injunction in Pakistan: Protecting a Legal Right Through Civil Relief

An injunction is a preventive remedy intended to restrain conduct that may violate or threaten a legally protected right. Courts do not grant permanent injunctions simply because a dispute exists; the claimant must satisfy the legal requirements for the relief sought.

When an injunction may be relevant

Injunction proceedings may arise in property, contractual, possession, business and other civil disputes where a party seeks to prevent a particular act or interference.

Right, threat and evidence

A claimant should identify the legal right requiring protection, the conduct that threatens or violates it, and the evidence supporting the claim. Documents, notices, photographs, records and witness evidence may be relevant depending on the dispute.

Interim versus permanent relief

Interim relief is temporary and is considered during the case, while a permanent injunction is part of the final adjudication. The requirements and practical purpose of each should not be confused.

Limits of injunction relief

An injunction cannot be used to obtain relief that the law does not permit or to bypass another required remedy. The court may consider whether damages or another remedy is appropriate and whether the claimant has established the necessary legal entitlement.

Key takeaway

The Specific Relief Act, 1877 contains important rules concerning preventive relief and injunctions. The precise remedy should be selected after reviewing the underlying right, facts and available evidence.
