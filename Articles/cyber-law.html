Cyber Law in Pakistan: Online Harassment, Unauthorized Access and Digital Evidence

Digital disputes can involve social-media accounts, private communications, impersonation, unauthorized access, harmful content and other technology-related conduct. Because digital evidence can be changed or deleted quickly, early preservation is important.

What can become a legal issue online?

Potential issues include unauthorized access to information systems, unlawful interference with data, impersonation, harmful online content and other conduct regulated by Pakistan's cybercrime framework. The Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act, 2016 is a key federal statute and has been amended over time.

Preserve digital evidence

Keep original messages, URLs, emails, account details, screenshots and relevant device information. Where possible, preserve the original files and metadata rather than relying only on a cropped screenshot. Do not alter or fabricate evidence.

Reporting and legal strategy

The correct reporting or court route depends on the alleged conduct and current law. A lawyer can help organize the evidence, identify the legal issue and determine which authority or proceeding may be appropriate.

Privacy and safety

Victims should avoid escalating an online dispute by making threats or publishing private information about the other party. Account security measures, preservation of evidence and professional legal advice are generally safer than retaliatory action.

Key takeaway

Cyber cases can involve rapidly changing technology and law. The 2016 Act and subsequent amendments should be checked in their current form before relying on a particular provision or procedure.

Legal framework: The Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act, 2016 has been amended over time; the current text and applicable rules should be checked for a live case.
